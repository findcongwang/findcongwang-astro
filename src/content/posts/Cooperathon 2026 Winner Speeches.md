## 1. University Research Track: Perceptiosphere (R.O.M.A.)

Thank you.

Last October, my mentor and father figure collapsed in front of our common students. He died very suddenly and unexpectedly. Wayne was a super-connector. He wove together an entire innovation community.

That created a negative space. A vacuum where an entire intellectual legacy used to be. The connective tissue disappeared overnight.

Every institution has a Wayne. Most will never know what they lost.

We have a societal crisis of knowledge succession. When people leave through retirement, departure, or death, knowledge leaves permanently. And it's not just tacit knowledge that disappears. Doctoral theses, symposium proceedings, decades of institutional practice sometimes sit publicly available but are never discovered. Because they are not mapped.

Long-term endeavours require cross-generational collaboration. People building on what came before rather than rediscovering it. When we reduce knowledge loss, we reduce waste. When we enable that collaboration across generations, ideas compound instead of resetting.

We are building a managed platform that captures living archives of knowledge into navigable context. We have built a living constellation of Wayne's body of work, connected to the broader knowledge in his domains. We are capturing stories to fill that negative space while we still remember. So that we can leave it to future generations.

We offer this as a built-to-manage service. If you know of a body of work that deserves to be mapped, if your institution is losing knowledge, or if you know of something worth preserving intellectually, come find me.

Thank you.

## 2. Planet Track: Climate Action Knowledge Map (Trusted Circle)

Thank you.

Millions of practitioners worldwide are adapting to climate change right now. Permaculturists, regenerative farmers, natural builders, indigenous land stewards. Each community holds extraordinary knowledge validated by decades and sometimes generations of practice.

We have both slow-gathered traditional knowledge accumulated over millennia and fast-paced scientific discovery producing new findings every week. But they are not mapped together. They are not connected. The missing bridge between how these traditions relate to each other is what blocks us from benefiting from this knowledge across disciplines and domains of practice. Something validated in one region could save another community ten years of trial and error. But it stays isolated.

We are building an open-access knowledge commons where these practices are decomposed into first principles and made composable. Searchable by bioregion, soil, and climate. When we understand WHY a practice works, we can transfer it, adapt it, and combine it with knowledge from other traditions.

But the richest ecological knowledge, held by indigenous communities for millennia, will not enter any system unless knowledge holders trust it. Equity of access and fairness of access are table stakes. So we built a sovereignty mechanism where communities define what to share, govern who accesses it, and can withdraw at any time. Think of it as peer-to-peer library subscriptions to knowledge that can be revoked. Made possible by our infrastructure built on knowledge composability.

When contribution is safe, the knowledge commons becomes comprehensive. Sovereignty enables collaboration. The invitation to contribute is open.

Thank you.

## 3. Prosperity Track: Sovereign Distributed Energy and AI for Canada (DAI Compute)

Thank you.

When a Canadian company uses Claude, AWS Bedrock, or Google Cloud, that workload runs on foreign infrastructure subject to the US CLOUD Act. Even the data centres physically in Quebec and Ontario are owned by American companies. Our federal government's own AI strategy acknowledges that Canadian firms compete on infrastructure they do not own, govern, or influence.

Every sensitive AI workload, civilian or defence, routes through foreign cloud. That is a sovereignty crisis.

We are building a decentralised AI compute platform backed entirely by Canadian-owned energy and hardware assets. We use blockchain to let Canadian citizens invest directly in physical compute nodes powered by renewable energy. The same sovereign network that processes commercial AI can serve Canadian defence and intelligence without data ever leaving Canadian soil. And because it is distributed across the country, there is no single point of failure. That is a defence characteristic you cannot get from a data centre.

But this is part of something larger. This is a 50-year project. Canada has the cheapest clean energy in the world, and a geography that becomes increasingly strategic as climate-driven migration pushes populations northward. That influx of population brings practice-tested business models, particularly from Asia, that are not viable here at our current scale but will become possible as our workforce and market density grows. We can be preparing now for that future: building the production capacity and supply chains necessary for vertical integration. Canadian-owned energy, Canadian-owned hardware, and eventually Canadian-manufactured chips.

We are building economic sovereignty from the ground up. By citizens. For the long term.

Thank you.
